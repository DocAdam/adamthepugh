Writing Philosophy

I care most about writing that helps someone do something, understand something, or decide something.

That sounds obvious, but a lot of documentation still gets shaped more by internal habits than by user need. It ends up too vague, too bloated, too jargon-heavy, too product-centered, or too disconnected from the actual moment when someone needs help.

I try to avoid that.

What I optimize for​

Usefulness​

The first question I care about is simple:

Does this actually help?

Not:

  • does it sound polished
  • does it sound technical
  • does it sound official

Useful writing wins.

Clarity without flattening everything​

I like clear writing, but not fake simplicity.

Some subjects are genuinely complicated. My job is not to pretend they are trivial. My job is to make them easier to follow, easier to navigate, and easier to act on.

Structure matters as much as sentences​

Bad documentation is often not bad because every sentence is terrible. It is bad because:

  • the information is in the wrong place
  • the page type is wrong
  • the task flow is unclear
  • the hierarchy is weak
  • the terminology is inconsistent
  • the content is hard to scan

That is why I think about structure as part of writing, not as a separate concern.

Respect for the reader​

I do not like wasting the reader’s time.

I want pages that get to the point, explain what matters, and reduce friction. That includes using examples, headings, definitions, and step structure where they actually help.

What good documentation usually needs​

In my experience, good documentation usually depends on some mix of these:

  • clear purpose
  • strong information hierarchy
  • well-scoped page types
  • consistent terminology
  • realistic examples
  • editing discipline
  • awareness of what the user is trying to do

How this shows up in my work​

This philosophy shows up in different ways across my work:

  • documenting complex hiring workflows for internal users at Meta
  • creating structured help from noisy community source material in TeraCreators Help
  • supporting teams with documentation strategy, quality, and consistency
  • organizing content around tasks, references, terms, and recurring user needs
  • using analytics and reporting to improve documentation decisions

The standard I care about​

The standard I care about is not “looks finished.”

It is:

  • easier to use
  • easier to find
  • easier to trust
  • easier to maintain
