UX and Help Content

UX and help content sits close to the moment where a user is trying to make sense of a system.

That makes it one of the most practical forms of writing. It has to be clear, well scoped, and useful under real conditions, often with very little room for waste.

What I focus on in UX and help content​

I care about:

  • whether the content meets the user where they actually are
  • whether the wording reduces friction instead of adding it
  • whether the terminology matches the product and the user’s mental model
  • whether the content is specific enough to help without becoming bloated
  • whether the structure supports the moment of use

Where this kind of writing shows up in my work​

This kind of writing has shown up in work like:

  • internal product documentation
  • support-oriented procedural content
  • recruiting workflow documentation
  • user-facing explanations of features and process behavior
  • task and help content built from messy source material
  • community-informed guidance that needed clearer structure and language

What makes good help content work​

Good help content usually depends on some mix of these:

  • a clear user need
  • a realistic sense of context
  • concise, direct wording
  • well-defined page boundaries
  • consistency with the product experience
  • awareness of likely confusion points

How I approach it​

I tend to ask:

  • What is the user trying to do right now?
  • What do they already know?
  • What are they most likely to misunderstand?
  • What is the smallest amount of content that will actually help?
  • Does this belong on the page, in the UI, in a task, or in reference content instead?

Those questions usually lead to better help.

Why this matters​

A lot of documentation is technically correct but still not very helpful.

UX and help content forces a more practical standard. The real question is not whether the content exists. It is whether it reduces confusion and helps the user move forward.
